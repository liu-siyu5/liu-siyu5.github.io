Tripster

What
A travel app that plans itineraries and matches travelling companions
When
January 2026 – present
Role
Product architecture, planning flow, matching model
State
Prototype in development, sixteen screens

Travel apps mostly ask where you are going and what you like, then return the top ten things in that city. The output is generic because the input is. Tripster is an attempt at asking better questions — and at matching people to travel with, not just places to go.

I own the product architecture: the data model, the planning flow, and what the system asks a traveller for before it suggests anything.

The design constraint I held to

Every suggestion the app makes has to carry the reason it was made. A recommended place says what about you it matched. A matched companion comes with the reasons, not only a percentage.

A number you cannot interrogate is not advice, it is an instruction — and people reasonably ignore instructions about their own holiday. A recommendation that gives its reasons can be disagreed with on the specifics, which is what makes it worth reading. It also means the app returns several plans rather than one, because the tradeoffs it asks about have no single right answer.

Details held back. This one is headed for release, so the planning model and the matching logic are not described here. Happy to talk through the architecture and the reasoning behind it.
